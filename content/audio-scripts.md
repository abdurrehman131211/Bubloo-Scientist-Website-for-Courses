# Bubloo Scientist Academy — Audio Scripts
## Course: AI Foundations — Your First 7 Days with AI

### Recording style
Warm, clear, energetic, professional. Aim for 5–8 minutes per lesson. Do not sound like a newsreader. Pause after questions so learners can think.

## Day 1 — What Is AI?
Welcome to Bubloo Scientist Academy. Today we are starting with one question: what actually is artificial intelligence?

AI is a broad field of computing focused on systems that can perform tasks we associate with human intelligence. That can include recognizing patterns, understanding language, making predictions, or generating content.

You may hear the terms machine learning and generative AI. Machine learning is one approach to building AI systems by learning patterns from data. Generative AI focuses on producing new content such as text, images, audio, or code.

Here is the important idea for this course: AI is a tool. A tool can be powerful without being perfect. AI can produce useful results, but it can also make mistakes. Your job is not to blindly trust it. Your job is to direct it, evaluate it, and use it thoughtfully.

Your activity today is simple. Look around your digital life and identify three things that use AI. Then write down what you think the AI is doing.

Tomorrow, we will look at what happens when you actually communicate with an AI assistant.

## Day 2 — How AI Assistants Work
Today we are talking about AI assistants and prompts.

When you give an assistant an instruction, it uses the available context and patterns learned during training to generate a response. You do not need a secret formula to write a good prompt. Clear instructions usually beat complicated instructions.

A useful prompt can contain four things: your goal, the context, constraints, and the format you want.

For example, instead of saying, “Teach me Python,” you could explain that you are a beginner, want to learn variables and loops, want simple examples, and want a short practice exercise at the end.

One more rule is essential: AI can sound confident while being wrong. For important facts, verify the answer.

Your activity is to take one vague AI request you have used before and rewrite it with a clear goal, context, constraints, and output format.

Tomorrow, we turn AI into a research assistant — while keeping you in control.

## Day 3 — Research Smarter with AI
AI can be useful during research, but it should not replace verification.

You can use AI to brainstorm questions, organize notes, explain difficult concepts, summarize material you provide, and compare ideas. But when a claim matters, check it.

A strong research workflow is simple: generate possibilities, find reliable sources, compare evidence, then write your conclusion.

Pay attention to dates and source quality. When possible, go back to primary or authoritative sources rather than relying only on a summary of a summary.

Your activity today is to choose a topic you are studying. Write five questions you want answered. Then identify two kinds of sources you would trust.

The goal is not to become suspicious of every AI answer. The goal is to become good at knowing which answers need checking.

## Day 4 — Create with AI
Today is about creativity.

AI can help you brainstorm, outline, draft, transform, and generate variations. But the creator still makes the important decisions: what should exist, who it is for, what quality means, and what should be changed.

Think of AI as a creative collaborator, not an automatic final editor. Give it a direction, inspect the result, and iterate.

For Bubloo Scientist, you might ask for ten reel concepts, choose one, develop the hook yourself, ask for alternative explanations, and then edit the final script into your own voice.

Remember to follow the rules of the platform, school, workplace, or client when disclosure of AI assistance is required.

Your activity is to create a one-paragraph concept for a technology education reel.

## Day 5 — AI + Coding
Today we connect AI with programming.

AI can explain code, suggest implementations, create tests, identify likely bugs, and help you understand unfamiliar code.

But there is a trap: copying code you do not understand can make learning slower, not faster.

Instead, ask for small steps. Ask what each part does. Run the code. Read the error. Test the behavior. Then modify it yourself.

And protect sensitive information. Do not casually paste passwords, private keys, or confidential information into an AI tool.

Your activity is to have an AI assistant explain a tiny Python function line by line, then rewrite the function yourself.

## Day 6 — Build Your AI Workflow
Today we move from individual tricks to systems.

A workflow is a repeatable sequence. One useful model is Capture, Clarify, Generate, Verify, Edit, Save.

You can adapt this to studying, content creation, coding, or research.

The goal is not to use AI everywhere. The goal is to remove repetitive work while keeping human judgment in the parts that matter.

Create a small library of prompts that you actually reuse. When a prompt works well, save it. When it fails, improve it.

Your activity is to design your own five-step AI workflow for one task you do repeatedly.

## Day 7 — AI Safety & Next Steps
You made it to Day 7.

Before you continue, remember three rules. Protect private information. Verify important claims. Keep human judgment in the loop.

AI systems can be wrong, outdated, biased, or missing context. Responsible use means understanding those limits rather than pretending they do not exist.

Now choose one small project for your next step. It could be an AI-assisted study guide, a simple coding project, a content workflow, or something completely different.

Make it small enough to finish. Learning compounds when you build.
